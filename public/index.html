The typo museum in your pocket

The world is full of typos. Someone has to collect them.

Snap a funny sign, menu or price tag. Typo Hunter reads the text, spots what's wrong, and adds it to your own museum of language mistakes. Then challenge your friends, or turn it into a class activity.

How it works

  1. 1

    Snap it

    Spot something funny? Tap the yellow + and take a photo of the sign, menu or label.

  2. 2

    The app reads it

    Typo Hunter reads the text on your phone, highlights words that look wrong and suggests what they meant. You check it and fill in the rest.

  3. 3

    Collect and compete

    Earn points for well-documented finds, keep your favourites in your Hall of Fame, and battle friends phone to phone.

Who it's for

Collectors

You already photograph funny signs, then lose them in a camera roll of thousands. Keep them in one place, with the story behind each one.

Friends on the move

Turn a walk or a trip into a game. Quick Duels, timed Speed Blitz rounds and squad battles work screen to screen by QR code, with no internet needed.

Teachers and classrooms

Run a class typo hunt in minutes. Students join with one code, hunt during the lesson and hand in their photos, and you approve each one.

Language learners

Working out why something is wrong is a great way to learn what's right. Every find is a tiny lesson.

Travellers

Hotel signs, airport notices and translated menus are a goldmine. Capture them offline and keep the best as souvenirs.

Spotted by hunters

Typos near you

Hunters can choose to share a find. Only the words and the city are public, never the photo or an exact location, and anyone can flag a post that shouldn't be there.

No public typos yet. Open the app, spot one, and tick "Let everyone see it?" to be the first.

Private by design

  • No account. Open it and start hunting.
  • Your photos stay on your phone. Text reading happens on the device, too.
  • No tracking. No ads, no analytics, and the app contacts no third parties.
  • You decide what to share. Nothing leaves your phone unless you show a QR code, send a file, or choose to post a find's words publicly.
